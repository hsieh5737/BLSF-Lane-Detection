PROPOSED MANUSCRIPT TEXT FOR AUTHOR AND EDITOR REVIEW
These paragraphs describe the corrected candidate, not a recovered historical
implementation. They require an explicit version reference in any correction.
They do not establish that the original reported results remain valid.

Section 2.2: angle convention and voting

Let the BEV image x-axis point rightward and its y-axis downward. For endpoints
(x1,y1) and (x2,y2), let dx=x2-x1 and dy=y2-y1. We define the undirected
segment orientation as theta=wrap_[-90,90)(atan2(dx,-dy)*180/pi). Vertical
segments have theta=0 regardless of endpoint order. Positive theta denotes a
rightward displacement as y decreases. Zero-length or nonfinite segments are
excluded. The length criterion is tau_i>T1.

In the corrected candidate, the closed voting bands are A0=[-35,0],
A1=[-5,5], and A2=[0,35] degrees in BEV pixel coordinates. Overlapping
membership is retained: a segment can contribute its length to more than one
band score. Only segments with |theta|<=35 degrees are admissible; this
overall envelope is fixed. The frame is assigned a low-confidence state when
all bin scores remain below an absolute threshold, max(B0,B1,B2)<Bmin. Because
the selected band coincides with the winning bin, max(B0,B1,B2) equals the
total length of the segments passed to the tracker, so overlapping membership
cannot cause or prevent rejection. A low-confidence state is not a verified
absence of painted lanes. No dominance-ratio criterion is applied: since A0 and
A2 jointly cover the admissible domain, max(B0,B1,B2) is at least half of the
unique eligible length in every frame. Ties prefer A1, then A0, then A2. The
selected band's closed bounds are used without additional expansion. These
conventions explicitly specify the corrected implementation and should not be
presented as recovered historical experimental settings.

Section 2.2: tracker input

Let B be the binary foreground after median local thresholding and S the
0/255 rasterized mask of retained segments. The corrected default uses
F=S as the input to peak detection and sliding-window tracking. It does not
use F=B union S. Rasterization, line thickness and rounding can make S contain
pixels outside B; thus S subset B is not assumed. The alternative F=B
intersection S has different semantics and must be separately identified and
evaluated. Low-evidence rejection produces no current-frame lane prediction
and does not invoke an unfiltered-binary fallback.

Sections 2.3 and 2.5: operating scope

The geometric prior applies only when the retained lane evidence is sufficiently
represented within the selected global angular band after calibrated BEV
mapping. The overall 35-degree envelope is a necessary admissibility condition;
it is not a guarantee that all orientations within that envelope are retained,
because the selected straight band is only [-5,5] degrees. A downstream
sliding-window tracker can follow the surviving evidence but cannot recover
segments removed by BLSF. Translating an axis-aligned window does not rotate
its coordinate system. Strong curvature, incorrect IPM, nonplanar surfaces,
unfavorable pixel scaling, occlusion and dominant lane-like clutter can violate
these assumptions. Local processing before orientation filtering would constitute
a distinct method variant requiring separate evaluation.

Section 4.2: geometric angle and curvature

Pixel orientation and physical road heading must be distinguished. Under an
ideal metric BEV with image scales sx and sy in pixels per metre,
tan(theta_pixel)=(sx/sy)*dx_metric/dy_metric. Consequently the physical heading
limit corresponding to a pixel-angle limit alpha_pixel is
alpha_metric=atan((sy/sx)*tan(alpha_pixel)), subject to valid mapping and the
chosen angular band. If sx=sy, heading and pixel angle agree.

For a circular lane, zero initial heading and an isotropically scaled metric
BEV, a look-ahead arc length S implies a heading change S/R. An angular
envelope alpha therefore yields the necessary relation R>=S/alpha, with alpha
in radians. If the look-ahead variable instead denotes the forward coordinate
distance Y, the corresponding circular geometry gives R>=Y/sin(alpha).
At 40 m and 35 degrees, these illustrative bounds are approximately 65.5 m
and 69.7 m, respectively. They are conditional geometric illustrations, not
validated universal detection limits. Nonzero initial heading, yaw error,
anisotropic scaling, the selected band and surviving support alter the
applicable condition.

For an axis-aligned tracker with forward step dy, the lateral motion contains
a first-order tangent term. For locally circular geometry away from
cos(psi)=0, it can be approximated in ground coordinates by
dx=tan(psi)*dy + dy^2/(2R*cos(psi)^3).
After conversion to the actual pixel scales, the magnitude of the displacement
and uncertainty must lie within the available capture margin. A curvature-only
sagitta bound applies in a tangent-aligned frame under its assumptions, not
automatically in a translated axis-aligned BEV window. Initialization and
support sufficiency remain necessary and this approximation is not a detection
guarantee.

Section 4: paired ablation definition for a NEW experiment

The corrected candidate and its no-BLSF control share the same preprocessing,
LSD extraction, minimum-length rule, mask rasterization, peak detection,
tracking and fixed-seed RANSAC settings. The ablation disables angular
voting/gating and the associated low-evidence rejection together. Its effect
therefore measures the entire BLSF stage. Separating the angular-filter and
frame-rejection contributions requires an additional pre-specified control.
Every failed or rejected frame remains in the evaluation list. No parameters
are selected using the test set. Results from this experiment must be labelled
as new results and linked to a version, configuration, frame manifest, prediction
files and exact evaluator.
