We agree that the released C++ snapshot (830a62f5eaf4bd1d74bc3be11451881cc3c9a0cf) does not consistently implement the geometry and feature path described in the article.

1. Orientation convention and bounds

The published BEV discussion and Figure 7 use a direction close to the image vertical as approximately zero degrees. The released code instead calculates an angle relative to the horizontal axis. In addition, its nonzero minimum absolute angle would exclude near-vertical segments after an axis correction. A corrected candidate implementation now uses an undirected angle relative to the upward BEV vertical, normalized to [-90, 90) degrees, with endpoint-reversal invariance. It includes zero, disables the 12-degree band expansion in the released implementation, and restricts acceptance to the selected published band within an overall absolute limit of 35 degrees.

The overlapping voting bands also require explicit treatment. With the released sum-of-bin-scores denominator, a vertical segment can be counted in all three bins and still fail the dominant-ratio threshold. The candidate retains overlapping bin membership and removes the ratio test. Because the left and right bands jointly cover the full 35-degree envelope, the dominant score is always at least half of the unique eligible length, so a ratio threshold of 0.40 could never reject a frame. The candidate instead applies the absolute evidence condition stated in the article: a frame is low-confidence when every bin score is below the threshold. The winning score equals the total segment length passed to the tracker. The straight band is preferred in an exact tie. These are explicitly documented repair choices.

2. Tracker feature support

We agree that B OR S retains every original foreground pixel. The corrected default passes the rasterized retained-segment mask S to peak detection and tracking, without union with the original binary image or fallback after low-evidence rejection. Because rasterization can create support outside B, we do not assume S is a subset of B. A separate B AND S option has different semantics and is not treated as interchangeable.

In local controlled tests using OpenCV C++ 4.6.0, an independently constructed two-rectangle binary image produced four long segments: the unchanged released filter retained none, whereas the corrected candidate retained all four. Twenty-one C++ regression cases and two diagnostic-evaluator tests passed, including endpoint reversal, angular and length boundaries, overlapping votes, the evidence threshold, mask support and a synthetic full-pipeline check. These tests do not constitute exact replication of the supplied OpenCV 4.13.0 test artifacts or reproduction of Tables 6 and 7.

The relationship between the released snapshot and the historical experimental artifacts underlying those tables remains unresolved. We therefore cannot presently state that the published numerical results are unaffected, or attribute the discrepancies to an earlier correct implementation without supporting records. The necessary reconciliation requires the experimental revision, complete settings and homographies, frame lists, annotations, per-frame predictions and exact evaluation scripts.

The geometric limitations also need clarification: a downstream sliding-window tracker cannot recover segments already discarded by the global filter, and a pixel-angle limit alone does not establish a universal minimum road radius. We will seek editorial guidance on the appropriate correction with the available evidence and the remaining limitations clearly identified.
